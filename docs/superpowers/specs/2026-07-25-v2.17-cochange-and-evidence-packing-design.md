Two approaches internalized from a critical review of `repowise-dev/repowise` (AGPL-3.0 — **ideas only,
no code copied**). Six other candidates were evaluated and **rejected on our own data**; the rejections
are recorded below so we do not re-litigate them.

**Revision r6** — r1 was corrected by the Phase-1A archaeology (three falsified claims); r2 was then
reviewed by Codex (gpt-5.6-sol, xhigh), which returned **DO NOT BUILD** with 2 CRITICAL + 6 HIGH.
Every finding was independently verified before acceptance; **all 13 were real**. Codex then reviewed
r3 and returned **DO NOT BUILD** again — 11 of 13 closed, 1 partial, 1 not closed, plus 10 defects the
rewrite itself introduced. r4 closes those. Corrections are marked `[r2]` / `[r3]` / `[r4]`.

**[r4] Convergence discipline (stated so it is not re-litigated).** Two of Codex's r3 fixes are adopted
in *reduced* form and one sub-part is **declined**, deliberately:
- *Reduced:* the verdict-freeze (r3 #4) is a prose ordering constraint in the reviewer, not a new
  mechanical post-processor outside it — the heavier design buys little against an LLM reviewer that
  could ignore either.
- *Reduced:* the evidence-prioritization algorithm is pinned to the degree that two implementers produce
  the **same** evidence (enum members, failure predicate, context radius, allocation order,

- *Declined:* percentile-based / repo-configurable commit-width thresholds (r3 #9). v1 ships fixed
  conservative defaults and **declares the portability limit in writing** (below). Making the bar
  tunable before we have a second repo's data would be fitting a knob to an imagined shape.

## Feature A — Co-change advisory (the inverse of the scope gate)

**The gap.** Our scope gate answers *"did a worker write OUTSIDE its lane?"* — a containment question.
It cannot answer the opposite and equally real failure: *"does a partition cover file A but FORGET
partner file B, which our own history says always moves with it?"*

**The statistic [r3] — ordered rules, not a symmetric pair.** r2 required
`min(P(B|A), P(A|B)) >= 0.70`. Codex showed this is the wrong shape: the question is *"A is in the
partition — is B missing?"*, an **ordered** rule `A → B`. Requiring the reverse direction suppresses
exactly the asymmetric relationships that matter. Worse, r2's bar was not robust on this repo's commit
shape: most co-changes of a passing pair sat in wide release commits.

**The rule a pair must clear, per direction:**

| Requirement | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| joint support | `>= 8` | a handful of co-occurrences is not a rule |
| point rate `P(B\|A)` | `>= 0.70` | the rule must usually hold |
| **95% Wilson lower bound** | `>= 0.50` | **[r3]** guards small-sample luck: `8/11` looks like 0.73 but its lower bound is 0.43 |
| **narrow support** | `>= 3` | **[r3]** at least three co-changes in **non-release, non-format commits touching <= 10 files** — a pair that only ever co-occurs inside a wide release commit is an artifact of the release, not a real coupling |
| release / bulk counts | reported | never hidden inside the headline number |

**Measured at HEAD (374 non-merge commits since 2026-01-01; largest commit 33 files):**

| Ordered rule | P | Wilson LB | narrow | release | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `marketplace.json` → `plugin.json` | 1.00 (34/34) | 0.90 | 6 | 24 | **fires** |
| `plugin.json` → `marketplace.json` | 0.94 (34/36) | 0.82 | 6 | 24 | **fires** |
| `plugin.json` → `CHANGELOG.md` | 0.92 (33/36) | 0.78 | 4 | 25 | **fires** |
| `marketplace.json` → `CHANGELOG.md` | 0.91 (31/34) | 0.77 | 4 | 23 | **fires** |
| `epic-mode.md` → `commands/v-epic.md` | 0.94 (17/18) | 0.74 | 17 | 0 | **fires** |
| `commands/v-epic.md` → `epic-mode.md` | 0.81 (17/21) | 0.60 | 17 | 0 | **fires** |
| `GEMINI.md` → `AGENTS.md` | 1.00 (8/8) | 0.68 | **2** | 1 | **rejected — bulk-confounded** |
| `AGENTS.md` → `GEMINI.md` | 0.73 (8/11) | **0.43** | 2 | 1 | **rejected — lower bound** |

**[r3] Two honest corrections to r2's own headline.** First, the `AGENTS.md ↔ GEMINI.md` pair — r2's
lead example — **does not clear the bar**. Every one of its co-changes lives in a wide commit (median
14 files); it is an artifact of broad doc sweeps, not a coupling. The two files really have drifted
(134 diff lines at HEAD), but that is a **manually observed drift**, not a rule this feature would ever
have fired on, and the spec no longer claims otherwise. Second, r2 called ten unilateral touches
"violations"; **[r3]** they are **unpaired historical touches** — an unadjudicated signal, not verified
defects. Calling them violations assumed the conclusion, which is the fabricated-evidence pattern this
project exists to prevent.

**[r3] The generalization claim, corrected twice.** r2 narrowed it too far. Under the ordered rule,
`plugin.json → CHANGELOG.md` **does** fire (P=0.92, LB=0.78) — the pair r2 declared permanently silent
at `min(P)=0.59`. So the computed signal covers **both** hand-written CI guards
(`validate.yml:43-52` and `:54-81`). It still does not **replace** them: CI enforces at push time with
an exact rule, this advises at partition time from a statistical one. Both guards stay.

### Design

New `scripts/compound-v-cochange.py` — pure stdlib, git-derived, **zero model involvement**:

- `rules --repo . [--since <date>] [--min-support 8] [--min-rate 0.70] [--min-wilson 0.50]
  [--min-narrow 3] [--narrow-max-files 10]` — emit **ordered** rules as JSON. **[r4] Normal output
  contains ONLY firing rules.** r3 simultaneously demanded that `rules` reproduce the two rejected
  `AGENTS`/`GEMINI` directions *and* that sub-threshold rules stay silent — a straight contradiction.
  Rejections are visible only through an explicit `--explain-rejections` diagnostic mode that `check`
  never invokes and never consumes.
- **[r4] An incomplete scan is never a clean scan.** If git output was byte-capped, or history is too
  short to clear the support bar, the result is `complete: false` with an `insufficient_history` /
  `scan_incomplete` reason and **no rules are emitted at all** — dropping a truncated trailing line
  repairs syntax, not missing commits, and biased support/Wilson values presented as complete would be
  precisely the fabricated-evidence failure this project exists to prevent. A caller must be able to
  tell "no missing partners" apart from "we could not tell".
- **[r4] Declared portability limit.** `--narrow-max-files 10` is calibrated on this repo's commit
  shape. A young repo, a squash-merge-only history, or a wide monorepo will legitimately produce **no**
  rules — which surfaces as `insufficient_history`, never as a clean bill of health. Tunable thresholds
  are deliberately deferred until a second repo's data exists.
- `check --repo . (--manifest <path> | --patterns <list>)` — **[r4] takes the manifest or a glob list,
  not a literal file list.** r3's `--files` did not match how ownership is actually expressed:
  `write_allowed` holds globs like `scripts/**`, so a literal file list would miss every antecedent
  covered by a pattern, and shell expansion would additionally miss files that do not exist yet. Rule
  antecedents are tested against the patterns with the **imported** `glob_match`; the selftest covers
  exact paths, `*`, `**`, and a path absent at HEAD. **[r3] Exit status is 0 whether or not findings exist**; a non-zero exit is
  reserved strictly for an operational error (git failed, bad arguments). A finding is data, never a
  failure signal — this is what structurally prevents a caller from treating it as a gate.
- **[r2] No cache, no cache file.** r1 said to mirror `docs/superpowers/memory/churn-cache.json`; the
  archaeology proved that file **does not exist in this repo and is untracked** — the precedent is
  absent. Full computation over 372 commits is sub-second, so v1 computes on demand. No
  `formula_id`, no `head_sha`, no committed artifact, no shared resource. (YAGNI: add a cache only if a
  repo appears where the scan is actually slow.)

**Git contract — every branch decided explicitly (the archaeology showed these are not inheritable
from `compound-v-churn.py`, which mines `--numstat` for a different purpose):**

- **[r3] Rename detection is explicit (`-M`) — for hermeticity, NOT as a live bug fix.** r2 claimed
  `-M` corrects a fabricated pair. **Verified against real git: it does not.** `git log --name-only`
  already resolves renames by default (`diff.renames` has defaulted true since git 2.9, and is unset in
  this repo), so bare `git log` and `-M` behave identically here; only an explicit `--no-renames` — the
  flag `churn.py:255` passes for its own numstat reasons — produces the two-path artifact. We pass `-M`
  so the result is **immune to a repo that sets `diff.renames=false`**, and the claim is downgraded
  accordingly. Selftest covers default-threshold rename behaviour, a below-50%-similarity move (which
  `-M` does *not* unify), and asserts at the **production `--min-n`**, not at a lowered threshold that
  would only prove an artifact can exist.
- **[r3] Release and format commits are excluded from NARROW support by a built-in default, not only by
  the taxonomy.** r2 said format commits are dropped via the imported `_is_format_subject`. Codex showed
  that with no real taxonomy `load_churn_config` returns **empty format patterns too**, so an imported
  matcher with an empty list rejects **nothing** — r2 admitted the excludes were a no-op here but not
  the subject filter. v1 therefore ships a **tested built-in default subject pattern set** for
  release/version/format commits, unioned with any taxonomy-supplied patterns, and the built-in set is
  documented and covered by the selftest.
- **Excludes** come from `load_churn_config` — imported, so they stay genuinely single-sourced.
  **[r2] Stated plainly: on this repo that resolves to ZERO exclusions** (`.claude/` holds only
  `compound-v-impact-taxonomy.example.yaml`, and `load_churn_config:166-174` refuses the `.example`
  fallback by design). The excludes are a contract honored for downstream repos, not an effect here.
- **[r3] `compound_v_churn._run_git` is IMPORTED and called — not re-implemented.** r2 only said git
  must "route through" the supervisor, which invites an implementer who cannot edit `churn.py` to fork
  its 34-line launch wrapper. The requirement is now explicit: load churn through the sibling-import
  seam and call **its** `_run_git`. Never `subprocess.run(timeout=)` on git.
- `--no-merges`; the `capped` truncation flag is honored (a truncated trailing line is dropped).
- **A non-zero git exit is reported visibly**, not silently flattened into "no pairs" (the sibling's
  latent behaviour at `churn.py:259-260` is not inherited).
- **Partners that no longer exist at HEAD are filtered**, or `check` would name a deleted file forever.
- **[r3] Every output carries its provenance**: `head_sha`, the exact `since`/`until` bounds, the
  eligible-commit count, and the active filter configuration. r2's own measurement was already
  irreproducible — it said 372 commits where HEAD now has 374, and recorded no SHA.
- `glob_match` is **imported** from `compound-v-taxonomy.py`, never forked — a third glob copy is
  exactly what CONVENTIONS.md forbids.

**[r2] ONE consumption point, the safe one.** r1 wired the signal into both the partition reviewer and
Review-Gate Pass 3. The archaeology found that `agents/spec-reviewer.md` has **no WARN vocabulary at
all** — every finding is an `ISSUE:`, and `:14` / `:203` make any `ISSUE:` a hard DONE-blocker enforced
by `commands/v-collect.md:41`. Wiring an advisory correlation in there would have **blocked every merge
on a correlation**. v1 wires **only** `agents/partition-reviewer.md`; the Review Gate is not touched.

**[r3] The advisory guarantee is now STRUCTURAL, not a promise.** r2 claimed the existing
`WARN: BATCHING_MISSING` sits "outside the FAIL block". **Verified: false** — it sits *inside* the
`[If FAIL, one section per failure code]` region at `partition-reviewer.md:93`, before `[If PASS:]`.
r2's guarantee therefore rested on a misreading. Three concrete changes replace it:

1. An **unconditional `WARNINGS` section** is introduced in the reviewer's output template, rendered
   for **both** PASS and FAIL, structurally separate from the FAIL codes.
2. `check` **exits 0 on findings**; non-zero means only an operational error. **[r4] Codex is right
   that an exit code alone cannot bind an LLM reviewer**, so it is paired with an ordering rule: the
   reviewer **determines and writes `VERDICT` BEFORE the co-change step runs**, and the co-change step
   may only append to `WARNINGS`. A verdict already on the page cannot be revised by a correlation.
3. The reviewer doc states explicitly that **neither a co-change finding nor an unavailable co-change
   scan may change `VERDICT`** — the verdict vocabulary stays `PASS | FAIL`, derived solely from the
   existing validator and failure codes.

**[r3] "No NEW hard gate", not "the only hard gate".** r2 wrote that the scope gate "remains the only
hard gate". That contradicts the code: the manifest validator (`partition-reviewer.md:38-40`), the
lockstep guards (`validate.yml:48`) and the selftest loop (`:279`) are all hard gates today. The
accurate claim: **co-change adds no new hard gate.**

**Anti-ruflo, non-negotiable:** counts and measured frequencies only, never a fabricated "risk score";
every warning carries support, the rate, the Wilson lower bound, narrow support and the window. It never
blocks and never edits. **[r4]** The earlier line claiming the scope gate is "the only hard gate" is
**deleted** — it contradicted the correction three paragraphs above and an implementer could not follow
both. The accurate statement, once: **co-change adds no new hard gate.**

## Feature B — Failure-prioritized evidence packing for cross-model prompts

**[r3] Renamed.** r2 promised "bounded AND never drops a failure line". Codex showed those are
**mutually impossible**: if the failure lines alone exceed the budget — or every line begins `ERROR` —
the function must either blow the budget or drop failure content, and a promise it cannot keep is a
prompt-budget denial of service waiting to happen. The guarantee is now **failure-prioritized and
explicitly lossy**, with defined overflow behaviour.

**The gap.** Two oversized-prompt incidents with external judges: a Codex review timed out at exit 124
as the repo grew (v2.6.2), and Sol went flaky at exit 144 on heavy repro prompts (v2.12) — both patched
by hand-tightening one prompt. The three truncators that exist today (`_bound_text`
`epic-arbiter.py:770-774`, `_cap_bytes_with_marker` `:608-620`, the caps at `:107-127`) are **all
tail-drop**, so they amputate exactly the traceback at the end of a log.

### Design

`pack_evidence(sanitized_text, budget_bytes, section_label)` in
`scripts/compound-v-collect-results.py`, imported by the arbiter through the **existing**
`_load_sibling_module` seam (`epic-arbiter.py:526-541`).

- **[r3] CRITICAL — it runs AFTER redaction, never before.** r2 ran the packer first and made markers
  path-free, believing that closed the egress hole. It does not: a packer that keeps
  `ERROR: <short secret>` while discarding the surrounding `BEGIN/END` or label lines **destroys the
  structure `redact()` matches on**, and a short secret then evades the >=32-char opaque-token regex
  too. Verified against `redact()` (`:641`), which matches labelled secrets and whole private-key
  blocks and fails closed only on an *unclosed* block. **Resolution:** split `redact()` into
  `redact_uncapped(text)` (all substitutions, fail-closed, **no cap**) plus the existing byte cap;
  `redact()` keeps its current signature and behaviour for every existing caller. The arbiter then does
  `redact_uncapped` → fail closed if `None` → `pack_evidence` on the **already-sanitized** text.
  Packing only *deletes* lines from sanitized text, so it cannot un-redact anything.
  **[r4] The exception boundary is specified, not left to the extraction.** Today ONE `try` covers
  conversion, every substitution, unclosed-block detection AND `_cap_bytes_with_marker`; a naive split
  could pass `None` into the cap or let a cap exception escape. Contract: `redact_uncapped()` catches
  conversion + substitution + unclosed-block failures and returns `None`; `redact()` propagates a `None`
  unchanged and **separately** catches cap exceptions. Regression tests inject a substitution failure
  and a cap failure, and cover an unclosed PEM block and a quoted secret.
- **[r3] `section_label` is a fixed enum**, not free text — an arbitrary label can itself carry a path.
- **[r3] Original order is preserved; nothing is reordered.** r2's global "failures first" would move a
  failure away from its job/section header and make an unrelated error look like it belongs to the
  failing feature — and the judge **votes on that altered association**. Prioritization decides *which
  spans get budget*; retained spans are emitted **in original order with their headers**, and omission
  markers sit **at the original gaps**. r2 also claimed a judge "can ask" for omitted detail; verified
  false — the prompt demands a single JSON object and defines no follow-up protocol.
- **[r4] The prioritization is pinned enough to be reproducible.** r3 left the failure predicate, span
  boundaries, context size, allocation order and the "fixed enum" undefined, so two implementers could
  hand a judge materially different evidence — unacceptable when the judge **votes**. v1 fixes:
  the `section_label` enum members; the failure predicate (an explicit, documented matcher list);
  a span = the matched line plus a fixed context radius; allocation order; and a deterministic
  tie-break. **[r5] The constants are fixed HERE, not left to the implementer** — Codex correctly noted
  that r4 named the categories without supplying values, so under a one-span budget two literal
  implementations could retain an early `ERROR` versus a later traceback and thereby elicit **different
  judge votes**:

  - `section_label` enum: `job_stderr`, `job_stdout`, `test_output`, `scope_gate`, `review_notes`,
    `diff_summary`.
  - Failure matchers (case-insensitive, anchored at line start after leading whitespace):
    `Traceback (most recent call last)`, `ERROR`, `FAILED`, `FAIL:`, `AssertionError`, `Exception`,
    `panic:`, `fatal:`, `E   `.
  - Priority rank: traceback block > `FAILED`/`FAIL:` > `ERROR`/`Exception` > `panic:`/`fatal:` >
    remaining matches.
  - Span = the matched line plus a context radius of **exactly 3 lines** each side. **Overlapping or
    adjacent spans are coalesced** into one before allocation.
  - **[r6]** Allocation walks priority ranks high to low and, within a rank, **original document order
    (earliest match position first) — this is the SOLE comparator.** r5 named two comparators
    ("earliest first" and "smaller byte length first") without saying which was primary, leaving which
    span survives a tight budget undefined. Match position is unique, so no further tie-break exists and
    none is needed. Encoded byte length is **not** a selection input.
  - **[r6]** When spans of **different** priority coalesce, the merged span takes the **highest
    contained rank**. r5 left this unspecified, which is a second way two implementations could retain
    different evidence.
  - Retained spans are emitted in **original document order** with their section headers.

  Not a full grammar — just enough that the output is a function of the input.
- **[r3] Defined overflow.** When prioritized content still exceeds the budget: keep section headers,
  keep bounded context windows around each retained failure, truncate an individual oversized failure
  span with an explicit marker, and check the final encoded size rather than hoping.
  **[r4] A TOTAL loss hierarchy, because r3's overflow rule was still mathematically incomplete** —
  "keep headers" and "emit a marker" and "fit the budget" cannot all hold when the headers alone exceed
  the budget, when the budget is smaller than a marker, or when template overhead leaves a non-positive
  budget. The ladder, applied in order until the result fits: (1) drop non-failure spans; (2) shrink
  context radius to zero; (3) truncate oversized failure spans with a marker; (4) truncate headers;
  (5) drop markers; (6) at a non-positive budget, emit the fixed placeholder
  `[evidence omitted: budget exhausted]`; (7) if even that does not fit, the arbiter **omits the
  evidence block entirely** rather than emitting an over-budget prompt. Every rung is reachable and
  individually tested.
  **[r5] Rung 7 needs its own integration path, and r4 did not give it one.** `_build_codex_prompt`
  turns a `None` evidence value into a non-empty placeholder that *keeps the evidence framing*
  (`epic-arbiter.py:802-804`), so "omit" via `None` would still emit a block and could still overflow
  when fixed template overhead alone exhausts the limit. v1 therefore introduces a **distinct
  budget-exhausted sentinel** that removes the evidence block from the template altogether, leaving the
  existing `None` behaviour untouched for every current caller. If the remaining fixed prompt *still*
  exceeds the limit, the arbiter **skips that poll** and records a bounded operational diagnostic —
  a missing ballot is honest; an over-budget prompt is a silent truncation of what a judge votes on.
- **[r3] Bytes end-to-end.** r2 budgeted characters while `redact` budgets UTF-8 bytes and
  `_build_codex_prompt` caps the whole prompt in characters — non-ASCII evidence could satisfy the
  character budget, exceed the byte budget, and get tail-cut again, **amputating the JSON response
  instructions**. v1 computes `evidence_budget_bytes = prompt_limit_bytes - encoded_template_overhead -
  marker_margin` and verifies the assembled prompt is within budget instead of truncating afterwards.
  **[r4] There is no circularity and no `assert`:** the template is split into **prefix and suffix**,
  both rendered and encoded FIRST, the exact remaining budget derived from them, packed once, assembled
  once. `marker_margin` is defined explicitly and **must not double-count** markers already charged
  inside `pack_evidence`'s budget. The final size check is an **explicit runtime comparison** — never a
  Python `assert`, which vanishes under `-O`.
- **[r3] Failure handling moves to the caller.** r2 said `pack_evidence` must never raise and must
  return its input unchanged. Both halves were wrong: `redact()`'s `except` (`:646`) cannot catch an
  exception from a *preceding* call, and returning the original oversized input silently restores the
  tail-drop behaviour with no signal that prioritization was lost. v1: the **arbiter** wraps the call
  and, on any packing failure, substitutes the bounded fixed placeholder
  `[evidence omitted: packing failed]` and logs locally. **Unbounded input is never returned.**
- Small inputs are byte-identical passthrough; the real pre-trim size is reported; the marker vocabulary
  is reconciled with the existing `TRUNC_MARKER` (`:129`) rather than becoming a third dialect.
- `collect-results.py`'s CLI contract (`main()` `:670-718`) is **unchanged**; new cases go in the
  existing `_selftest()` (`:533-626`) using its `check(name, cond)` idiom.

## Acceptance criteria

**Feature A**
- **[r6]** NORMAL `rules` output at HEAD emits **only the six firing rules** and nothing sub-threshold.
  `rules --explain-rejections` — a diagnostic mode `check` never invokes — additionally reproduces the
  two REJECTED `AGENTS.md`/`GEMINI.md` directions with the reason each was rejected (narrow support /
  Wilson bound). (r5 still demanded both from normal output; that was a contradiction.)
- Each emitted rule is **ordered** and carries: support, `P`, the 95% Wilson lower bound, narrow
  support, release/bulk counts, and the provenance block (`head_sha`, `since`/`until`, eligible-commit
  count, active filter config). No fabricated risk score, no confidence %.
- `check` **exits 0 whether or not findings exist**; non-zero is reserved for operational errors — a
  selftest asserts this, because it is what structurally prevents a caller from gating on it.
- Rules below any threshold are **silent**, never surfaced with a hedge.
- A non-zero git exit is surfaced, not flattened to "no rules"; partners deleted at HEAD are filtered.
- `_run_git`, `load_churn_config`, `_is_format_subject`/`_compile_format_patterns` and `glob_match` are
  **imported**; a selftest or a grep-style assertion proves no forked copy was introduced.
- Selftest fixtures (tempdir repo outside the worktree, `GIT_CONFIG_GLOBAL`/`SYSTEM=os.devnull`, pinned
  identity and dates) cover: default rename behaviour **at production `--min-support`**; a
  below-50%-similarity move; the built-in release/format subject defaults with **no** taxonomy present;
  a bulk-confounded pair rejected by narrow support; a small-sample pair rejected by the Wilson bound;
  a deleted-at-HEAD partner filtered; a non-zero git exit surfaced; `check` exit 0 with findings.

**Feature B**
- `redact_uncapped()` is extracted with `redact()`'s substitutions and fail-closed semantics intact;
  **`redact()`'s existing signature and behaviour are unchanged for every current caller**, proven by
  the arbiter's existing selftest still passing.
- A selftest asserts the ordering hazard directly: a private-key block whose `BEGIN`/`END` lines would
  be dropped by packing is **fully redacted** because packing runs on already-sanitized text.
